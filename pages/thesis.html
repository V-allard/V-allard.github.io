Superfluid dynamics in neutron stars

My PhD thesis is entitled Superfluid dynamics in neutron stars. It was conducted under the supervision of Prof. Nicolas Chamel (I.A.A. and BRU-ULB, Université Libre de Bruxelles). This work was financially supported by the Fonds de la Recherche Scientifique (Belgium) under Grants No. PDR T.004320. Abstract

Born from gravitational-core collapse supernovae, neutron stars provide a unique opportunity to explore matter under extreme conditions not reproducible on Earth. Their inner cores contain strongly interacting nucleons, forming highly correlated quantum states. Below a few times 108 K, the baryons become superfluid. These superfluid states influence various aspects of the star’s dynamics, including cooling, spin-down, and rotational irregularities known as “glitches.” This thesis develops a unified framework to study these superfluid states microscopically and macroscopically. Starting from zero-temperature generalized energy-density functionals, finite-temperature extensions are derived, capturing the properties of BCS superfluidity in homogeneous matter. This work reveals the presence of a dynamical gapless state, triggered by finite superfluid currents, which significantly affects the neutron specific heat. These microscopic results are incorporated into the framework of multi-fluid hydrodynamics to describe neutron star oscillations, entrainment effects, and glitch models. As a final application, the impact of this gapless state on the thermal relaxation of accreting neutron stars is investigated, with a focus on soft X-ray transients.

Summary

Neutron stars are born from the gravitational-core collapse of massive stars during supernovae. Initially hot, with temperatures up to 1011–1012 K, they cool rapidly and undergo a phase transition into a quantum liquid state. Below a few times 108 K, neutrons and protons form Cooper pairs and become superfluid, influencing the star’s dynamics. These superfluid phases are crucial for understanding the thermal evolution and rotational behavior of neutron stars, particularly the glitches observed in pulsars, which are thought to result from the differential rotation between the superfluid interior and the crust.

This thesis aims to model the thermodynamic properties of nuclear superfluidity in neutron stars and explore its macroscopic implications. Using generalized energy-density functionals extended to finite temperature, the study analyzes homogeneous superfluid matter under various thermodynamic conditions. A key finding is the existence of a gapless superfluid state under the influence of finite superfluid currents, relevant to rapidly rotating or accreting neutron stars. This state is shown to affect the specific heat and pairing properties significantly, providing insight into thermal relaxation and glitch dynamics.

These microscopic results are then embedded into a multi-fluid hydrodynamic framework to describe macroscopic phenomena such as entrainment, mutual friction, and angular momentum exchange. The approach is applied to oscillation modes and vortex-mediated glitch models. Finally, the implications for thermal relaxation in soft X-ray transients are investigated, showing that the presence of a gapless superfluid state can impact the cooling curves observed in systems like MXB 1659-29.
